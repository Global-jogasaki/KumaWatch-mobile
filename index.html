Supplementary diagnostics. The Precision@K, ROC-AUC, and daily case analyses below supplement the four-page proceedings paper. They are retrospective analyses of the same held-out 2025 labels and the released or deterministically regenerated daily scores. They do not constitute a post-deployment evaluation or evidence of causal operational benefit.
Evaluation protocolConditions behind every number

Top-K metrics change substantially with the averaging set and the value of K, so the conditions are stated once here rather than attached to each table.

Task
Global top-K: for each day, the K cells with the highest score across the whole prefectural grid. Grids are 144 cells (Yamagata) and 260 (Akita), each ≈10 km × 10 km.
Window
The held-out year 2025, 365 days. Model fitting uses data through 2024-12-31 only; recency features for day t use observations strictly before t.
K
10, 20 and 30. K = 20 is the patrol budget the paper evaluates.
Sighting days
Days with at least one reported sighting — 213 of 365 (Yamagata), 323 of 365 (Akita). Recall@K is only defined here, so this is the set the paper reports.
All days
All 365 days; a day with no sighting contributes a precision of 0. All-day and sighting-day values are computed and labelled separately; no reported aggregate combines the two day sets.
Ties
Ties are resolved by numpy.argpartition, matching the benchmark notebook. Tied cells have no semantic ordering at the K-th boundary; the exact selection depends on the versions pinned in requirements-diagnostics.txt.
Labels
A reported sighting, not occupancy. Every method predicts where sightings will be reported.
Coverage
The paper benchmarks eleven methods. These diagnostics cover ten of them: the four learned methods from released score matrices, and B0–B5 regenerated deterministically from Cell 5 of the benchmark notebook (RAND_SEED = 42). Poisson-GLM is the exception — no score matrix for it is released, and its published Recall@20 is 0.027.

ROC-AUCThree definitions, three questions

ROC-AUC is reported as a supplementary threshold-free diagnostic. It does not replace Recall@K or Precision@K, which correspond directly to the fixed daily patrol budget. “ROC-AUC” is not one number — these three are computed from the same scores and answer different questions.

Highlighted values mark the highest in each column. Pooled ranks all 52,560 cell-days at once. Daily cross-sectional ranks the 144 cells within each day and takes the unweighted mean over the 213 days with a mixed outcome — the closest of the three to the patrol decision. Per-cell temporal ranks the 365 days within each cell, an unweighted mean over the 90 cells that had both outcomes; it measures a different task from the one this benchmark scores.

Two readings that only appear once the definitions are separated. The static prior's pooled AUC of 0.827, despite having no temporal variation at all, illustrates that pooled cell-day AUC can be high without any ability to rank days within a cell — where it scores exactly 0.500. And Extra Trees exceeds TTM under pooled ROC-AUC (0.883 vs 0.842) while ranking below it on Recall@20 (0.474 vs 0.492). Which method looks better depends on the metric, which is the paper's argument for choosing the metric from the decision rather than from convention.
Retrospective comparisonHeld-out 2025 sighting reports · 10 methods

For each of the ten methods with released or deterministically regenerated 2025 cell-day score matrices, the repository publishes the predicted top-20 cells beside the cells that actually reported a sighting, decomposed into true positives, false positives and false negatives, with the pairwise top-20 agreement between methods. This produces 3,650 method-day rows per prefecture in results/daily_diagnostics_<pref>_2025.csv.

The cases below are selected automatically by the stated deterministic criteria rather than manually chosen for favourable visual appearance. The set intentionally includes both low-performing and relatively favourable cases.

Highlighted values identify the metric that triggered each deterministic selection criterion; they do not necessarily indicate model failure. On 2025-01-01, the single cell that reported a sighting was outside GLM-Logit's top 20 but inside the static prior's top 20. On a one-sighting day, Recall@20 is binary — either 0 or 1 — and is therefore highly sensitive to the rank of a single cell. It should not be treated as a stable estimate of overall model performance. On the highest-volume day, GLM-Logit and the static prior differ by only one captured sighting within the top-20 budget (14 versus 13 of 30). This is a single diagnostic case and does not establish a general relationship between sighting volume and model agreement.

What this is not. These are predictions compared retrospectively against held-out records. KumaWatch has not been evaluated in operation: no patrol routes, deployment period, control municipality or staff-decision records exist in this dataset, so no claim is made about patrol efficiency, injuries avoided or capture rates. Downstream uses of HierBayes posterior variance and of the Extra Trees environmental covariates are untested rather than refuted.
Benchmark result summaryYamagata, primary setting
GLM-Logit
Highest Recall@20 (0.547) among all eleven benchmarked methods, and highest Precision@20 (0.245) among the ten covered by these supplementary diagnostics. Its Recall@20 margin over the static prior is +0.014 and does not approach significance (p = 0.158), while the inexpensive seasonal baseline B5 is only 0.0009 behind on Precision@20. On Recall@20, the static prior — a method with no model, no features and no daily computation — is statistically indistinguishable from GLM-Logit in this setting.
HierBayes
Recall@20 = 0.542; no improvement over GLM-Logit on Yamagata (p = 0.621) at roughly sixty times the runtime. Posterior-uncertainty applications were not evaluated.
TTM
Significantly worse than the static prior on Yamagata (Δ = −0.041, p = 0.0004), at about four hours of API inference per day. On Akita it also trails, but not significantly (Δ = −0.010, p = 0.364).
Extra Trees
At Recall@20, Extra Trees underperforms the other non-ablation learned methods and is strongly miscalibrated (Brier Skill Score = −1.63). It nevertheless exceeds TTM under pooled ROC-AUC, illustrating that model ordering depends on the evaluation metric. Cross-method disagreement is not evidence of useful complementarity.
